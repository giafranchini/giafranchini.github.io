ARCHES: Autonomous Robotic Networks to Help Modern Societies

DLR planetary exploration analogue mission with an heterogeneous robotic team.

Overview

ARCHES (Autonomous Robotic Networks to Help Modern Societies) was a Helmholtz Association future project (2018–2022) led by the DLR Institute of Robotics and Mechatronics, with partners including the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), the Alfred Wegener Institute (AWI), GEOMAR and the European Space Agency (ESA) as associated partner. The project built the technical basis for heterogeneous, autonomous robotic networks, with application scenarios ranging from ocean monitoring and crisis intervention to the exploration of the Solar System.

The programme culminated in the space-analogue demonstration mission on Mount Etna, Sicily (13 June – 9 July 2022), where the volcano’s granular terrain and solidified lava layers made it a ‘quasi-Moon’ for testing future planetary exploration missions. A heterogeneous robot team, consisting of the RODIN lander, the LRU1 and LRU2 rovers and the ARDEA drone, operated under a mission operations centre in Catania and demonstrated cooperative geological exploration with remote spectral imaging, LIBS spectroscopy and autonomous sample selection and collection, astronaut teleoperation of ESA’s Interact rover with shared autonomy and haptic feedback, and the autonomous installation of a low-frequency radio antenna array, a key technology for future radio astronomy on the far side of the Moon.

My contribution

As a research intern at DLR (Jan–Jul 2022), within the space-analogue demonstration mission:

  • Developed high-level autonomy skills for multi-robot planetary exploration and mobile manipulation, based on RAFCON hierarchical state machines
  • Directly participated in the demonstration campaign on Mount Etna, supporting the multi-robot mission scenarios (Lehner et al., 2023; Prince et al., 2023)
